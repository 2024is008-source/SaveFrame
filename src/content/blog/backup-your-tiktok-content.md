A useful backup is a verified local copy you can find again. TikSaveMP4’s prepared files are temporary, so a browser tab or a saved download URL is not a long-term archive. Save the files themselves and keep your own original exports whenever possible.

## Start with your originals

Collect the recordings, full-size images and editing exports you created before upload. Keep platform downloads in a separate folder so you can distinguish a TikTok-delivered copy from the original master. Do not overwrite your only original just because a smaller downloaded version plays correctly.

## Collect the public post copies

Use the [video downloader](/video-downloader) for available MP4 files and the [photo downloader](/photo-downloader) for image posts. A photo ZIP is convenient for a complete slideshow, but extract and verify it before considering the job finished.

## Use a simple folder scheme

- Keep one folder for each project or post.
- Use descriptive names that include the project and date.
- Preserve numbered photo filenames when slide order matters.
- Add a small text note with the public post link and any permissions you need to retain.

Storing the source link is useful context, but it is not a replacement for the downloaded file. The post may later become unavailable.

## Verify and copy

Open every video and check its sound and ending. Compare photo counts and inspect the extracted files. Then make a second copy on a separate device or storage service you control. Test that you can open that copy instead of assuming a completed transfer is enough.

## Keep realistic expectations

A download does not preserve comments, engagement statistics, your editing timeline or the entire TikTok account. Downloaded quality also depends on what the source exposed. Read the [quality guide](/blog/tiktok-download-quality) when deciding which files should be treated as archival originals.
