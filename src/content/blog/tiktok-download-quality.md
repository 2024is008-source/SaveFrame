Download quality starts with the source TikTok makes available. A downloader can present those files and prepare supported tracks, but it cannot recreate detail that was removed during an earlier upload or compression step. Treat an available resolution as a property of the source, not a quality upgrade.

## Compare available formats

After inspecting a video in the [video downloader](/video-downloader), compare the returned dimensions and formats. Portrait and landscape clips have different shapes, so read both width and height rather than relying only on an HD label.

A larger file may contain more detail, a different codec or a different bitrate. It is not automatically a better recording. Use the preview to confirm the content, then judge quality from the saved file at a sensible viewing size.

## Preserve photo dimensions

The [photo downloader](/photo-downloader) saves the available source image rendition. A screenshot adds another capture step and may include interface elements. Saving an actual image file keeps the returned file dimensions and avoids that extra screen capture.

JPEG and WebP are image formats, not promises of identical quality. Avoid converting an image repeatedly if you do not need a different format; unnecessary lossy conversions can introduce additional artifacts.

## Know what cannot be recovered

Cropping, upscaling or selecting a different download option cannot reveal detail that is absent from every available source. The service also does not promise watermark removal. See [the watermark explanation](/blog/tiktok-download-watermarks) before treating a platform copy as a clean editing master.

## Keep your own originals

For content you create, retain the original video export, camera files and full-size photos. A downloaded platform copy is useful for reference and offline playback, while your originals remain the best starting point for editing and archival work.
