A TikTok video’s preview image does not tell you whether the downloadable file includes usable audio. When you need sound, inspect the actual video options and test the finished MP4. TikSaveMP4 can prepare available video and audio tracks together, but cannot supply a soundtrack the source does not expose.

## Inspect the video result

Paste the public post link into the [video downloader](/video-downloader). Confirm that the result is a video rather than a photo post. Choose an available video format; the public tools do not offer a separate music or MP3 downloader.

When a supported source provides separate tracks, preparation may include a merging stage. Keep the page open until the server finishes and the browser starts saving the file.

## Test playback locally

1. Open the saved MP4 from your device’s file manager.
2. Check the device volume and any muted-player control.
3. Play several parts of the clip, including the end.
4. Try another compatible player if the file behaves differently from other MP4 videos.

A silent player does not always mean the download omitted audio. Conversely, successful picture playback does not prove that an audio track is present.

## Understand missing sound

The source may be silent, the soundtrack may be unavailable outside TikTok, or the returned format may lack usable audio. Choosing a higher resolution cannot create a missing soundtrack. If you own the video, compare the downloaded copy with your original export.

## Photo slideshows are different

Saving the images from a photo slideshow does not save its in-app music as a separate file. The [photo slideshow guide](/blog/download-tiktok-photo-slideshows) explains what is included in image downloads. For preparation errors rather than silent playback, use the [troubleshooting guide](/blog/tiktok-downloader-not-working).
